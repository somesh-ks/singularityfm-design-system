## DS-13: a button hugs its words; only a glyph's slot is fixed, and a Play with no words is its glyph alone

- Status: active; amends DS-9 and DS-10
- Date: 2026-11-13
- Decision: the owner's call ("in general buttons should hug text"; "the button resizing problem should be fixed by keeping the icon size same and not by hardcoding the button size"; "the play button in the hero should be just the icon button, and the same in chapters"). A control is as wide as the words it says now, never as wide as words it might say.
  - A glyph slot stays one fixed box and the state picks the mark in it (DS-9 keeps this), so a mark that swaps moves nothing.
  - `reserve` on PlayChip, Button, Fab and Select is deprecated: it still works until 1.0.0 removes it, and nothing draws it. PlayChip loses its "Play again" default and the chip its 7rem floor; a played chip under a pointer swaps its check for the replay glyph and keeps its words.
  - PlayChip with no `label` is its glyph alone (`chip-glyph-only`): a circle as tall as the chip, its name in aria-label, every state in the one slot; preparing shimmers the glyph. The hero's Play and each story's are drawn this way, so the Play buttons that changed their words no longer have any.
  - A chip's digits are tabular in every state, so a time that ticks and the start of play never change the words' width.
  - `layoutStability.test.ts` checks that PlayChip, Button and Fab hug (the room around the words is the same for two words) and that the glyph-only Play keeps its box in every state.
- Context changed: DS-9 reserved the widest words a control might say. On a phone that read as a fixed width: a story's "0:22" sat in a pill as wide as "Play again", and the floating Play's words in one as wide as "Resume · 88 min left" (owner, 2026-11-13, with a screenshot).
- Rejected: easing the width when words change (the neighbours still move, and with no words on the Play buttons nothing changes); keeping the reserve as an opt-in for new work (it is the behaviour the owner rejected).
- Revisit if: a control must change its words in place beside others; then shorten or fix the words' shape before any reserve.
